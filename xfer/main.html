River Meditation · Prototype in progress

VR Experience

What if you could float down a meditative river in virtual reality?

Year2026
StageUnity prototype
RoleConcept, environment & interaction design
SoftwareUnity, ShapesXR
01

The vision

A raft carries you down a river at night. There’s nothing to steer and nothing to win. You breathe, look around and let the landscape come to you, next to someone you care about.

02

The challenge

Make relaxation and connection feel natural in a headset. No menus, no goals, no pressure to talk, and nothing that overwhelms someone at the end of a long day.

03

The experience

The raft sets the pace. A rainbow, a curtain of plants and animals drifting by appear one at a time along the banks. The ride ends at a hot spring where both people can step off and explore.

Design rationale

Nature, brought indoors

Studies of simulated nature show it can lift positive mood, and more immersive formats tend to show stronger effects (Li et al., 2023). Real nature still does more (Browning et al., 2020). The goal isn’t to replace the outdoors, but to bring some of that calm to people who can’t get there.

Design rationale

The river decides

When you’re worn out, even choosing where to go is work. The raft moves on its own, so there’s no navigation. Each moment is spaced along a straight path so attention stays soft instead of scattered. Night light and planets signal the end of the day, and somewhere that isn’t Earth.

Next iteration

Breathing, together

Slow breathing, under ten breaths a minute, is linked to higher heart rate variability and feeling more relaxed (Zaccaro et al., 2018). Moving in sync with someone tends to increase closeness (Mogan et al., 2017). Next, the river’s pace and light will follow both people’s breathing, so two people can fall into the same rhythm without saying a word.

Evolution

From ShapesXR to Unity

I sketched the world first in ShapesXR to test scale, pacing and the raft with two people in headsets, then rebuilt it in Unity with a new sky, lighting and a first-person ride.

Sources: Li et al. (2023), Frontiers in Psychology · Browning et al. (2020), Frontiers in Psychology · Zaccaro et al. (2018), Frontiers in Human Neuroscience · Mogan et al. (2017), Journal of Experimental Social Psychology
