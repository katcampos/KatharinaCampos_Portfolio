Object story · Accordion booklet + AR

MaracatuRhythm

A seven-panel booklet about the Caboclo de Lança, the lance-carrying guardian of Maracatu Rural from Pernambuco, Brazil.

Year2026
Format5×5 in accordion booklet + AR
RolePhotography, papercut, layout & stop motion
SoftwareInDesign, Photoshop, Artivive
01

The object

A miniature Caboclo de Lança, a gift from my mom. He comes from the sugarcane fields of Pernambuco, where the cutter’s tool became a lance and the lance became a dance of protection.

02

The story

For over a century, through exploited labor, poverty and neglect, he has kept walking into carnaval. The book is about that persistence: resistance carried as celebration.

03

The book

Photo and text panels alternate like call and response. Handmade glitter papercuts repeat across the pages like a beat, and the last panel comes alive in AR.

The sequence

Six images, six stages

The viewer meets him, gets pulled into his color and motion until he dissolves into carnaval, then comes back to him as a living archive. Crops and color grading build the energy, then let it settle. Each photo sits beside its text. DK Downward Fall, a hand-cut display face, bounces like the dance, and Charming Sixties adds the warmth of festival signage. The primary colors come from his chita fabric.

Motion extension · Artivive

The last panel moves

The final text, “Born from ordinary people, kept alive as collective resistance,” triggers an 11-second stop motion. The overlays in the book are handmade papercuts, so the animation is made the same way: flag, ribbons, mask and carnation slide in one by one while stars and spirals spin around him. The book builds energy, slows into still text, and ends in movement instead of a full stop, the way the tradition survives: by continuing.

Behind the work

01

The object & research

I looked closely at what he carries: the lança swung up and down as protection, the cravo in his mouth that closes the body against harm, the cheap and loud chita fabric worn as maximum presence. Then I took notes from interviews and documentaries about Maracatu Rural.

02

Photoshoot 1: glitter & flowers

A glitter set, silver-on-black studies, then ferns and dried flowers.

03

Photoshoot 2: movement & night light

Spin and zoom blur against glitter, then red, teal and magenta gels through haze after dark.

04

Glitter paper & handmade elements

I bought glitter paper and cut the Pernambuco and Brasil flags into pieces to reuse across the layout, plus elements that stand for the caboclo: spirals, stars, a sun and a paper rose. Each one was photographed, cut out and layered onto the pages.

05

Layout options & critique

I laid out frame options on the table and on the wall, with swap options under each one, and used the critique to lock the order of the six images.

06

Prototypes

Four versions, including a mini one, to test size, folding, page order and how the panels pair up when the book folds.

07

Stop motion for AR

I built the paper caboclo piece by piece under a ring light and shot it frame by frame. Those frames became the Artivive animation.

08

Final critique

Classmates unfolded the printed booklets and left notes.

What worked

  • It felt authentic
  • The movement in the narrative works
  • The handmade elements strengthen the story

What I’d push next

  • Make the green more harmonic with the rest of the sequence
  • Refine the first frame of the AR
  • Try other materials for the prototype
