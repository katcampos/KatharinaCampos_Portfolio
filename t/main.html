Product design · Camping nightstand

TuckNightstand

A rolling nightstand for people who camp on their own: light, sealed storage and wheels in one piece.

Year2026
FormatProduct concept + 3D renders
RoleResearch, concept, 3D modeling & presentation
SoftwareBlender, Illustrator, InDesign
01

Who it’s for

Monica, 65, camps with her dog and sometimes her grandkids. She sets everything up herself and sleeps on a cot. Independence is the point.

02

The problem

Her low points are physical: rolling gear over gravel with a leash in the other hand, bending down again and again, and finding her medication in the dark at 2 a.m.

03

The answer

Nothing on the market combined all three. TUCK handles light, storage and mobility in one rounded, rotomolded shell with nothing bolted on.

Every door openMirror lid · lit interior · side shelves · front deck
01 · Light

One light, always in the same place

The lantern sits flush in the lid so it can’t get knocked off in the car. Press it and it rises to light the bedside, easy to find by touch. It also lifts out to carry to the dog or the grandkids, and the ring clips to a tent loop to light the whole site.

02 · Storage

A place for everything

The front door is gasketed and drops down into a deck with a grip mat, so dirty shoes stay off the tent floor. Inside, two lit shelves keep first aid at cot height. Side shelves fold out on both sides with a recessed cup holder, so the dog can’t sweep a mug onto the ground.

03 · Vanity lid

Small things, never lost

The lid lifts to an LED-ringed mirror and a tray with fixed slots for medication, glasses and jewelry, each found by feel. The whole tray comes out and travels on its own.

04 · Mobility

No lifting, no bending

A telescoping handle lets her tilt it back and roll it with one hand while the other holds the leash. Set down, it stands at bedside height. Leveling feet twist down over stones and roots, so a bump won’t tip it over.

In context

Beside the cot

In the morning it holds coffee and the leash for the dog walk. At 2 a.m., the inhaler and meds sit under lantern light, within reach of both beds.

“I can still do all of this. I just can’t do it crouched on the ground in the dark.”

Monica Turner, 65 · persona

Process

From research to final form

I started with the person and the hard moments of one camping weekend. Every form decision traces back to one of those points.

01

Persona & journey map

One weekend with Monica, from packing the car to heading home. The low points (gravel, 2 a.m., packing down) became the three features.

02

Market research

I compared three competitors. None combined a light, sealed storage and wheels, and that gap became the brief.

03

Sketches

Five pen-on-paper ideas, each answering one pain point: wheels, a pole light, a vanity lid, two-way drawers and a comfy handle.

04

Version 8 & critique

By version 8 there were about 25 features. More parts meant more to carry, break and explain, so I cut it to three, done right, in a sturdier rounded shell.

05

Final parts

Seven parts in one object: vanity lid, organizer tray, side shelves, lit interior, drop-down deck, solid wheels and leveling feet.
